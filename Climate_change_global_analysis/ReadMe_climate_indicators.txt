The dataset contains data from years 1850 to 2025. The variables are indicators of climate change.

1) Solar irradiance TSI

https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/climate-data-records/total-solar-irradiance

It is measured in Watt per squared centimeter. It describes the amount of solar energy received per unit area at the top of Earth's atmosphere, on a surface perpendicular to the incoming sunlight. We get one measurement per year which is according to the dataset is performed on the 1st of January. The parameter TSI_UNC is the uncertainty.

https://www.ngdc.noaa.gov/stp/space-weather/solar-data/solar-indices/sunspot-numbers/cycle-data/table_cycle-dates_maximum-minimum.txt
here we can find solar minimum and maximum from each cycle.



2) Global ocean average temperature departure 

https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/monitoring/climate-at-a-glance/global/time-series/globe/ocean/tavg/1/9/1850-2026

The unit is degrees of Celcius. It shows the difference of the average ocean temperature on Earth with respect to a reference, the average between 1901-2000. Measurements took place every September.

3) Trends in atmospheric Carbon Dioxide

https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/access/monitoring/climate-at-a-glance/global/time-series/globe/ocean/tavg/1/9/1850-2026

It shows the CO2 mole fraction measured in the atmosphere. These values denoted the measured CO2, regardless of their source (human activity, volcanoe etc). It is the parameter which states the problem that is primarily responsible for the heating up of the earth. The data from 1959 and onwards are collected by the Mauna launa observatory with modern methods of measurement. Before that, the data is collected from the Antarctica, via the method of drilling ice cores and extracting the air enclosed there. Both scientific methods are precise and reliable and it is valid to combine their data in order to get a continuous trend.
